## Delivery decision

**Not ready to ship with a promise that a normal lighter flame receives lower operator priority.** In three fresh deployed lighter runs, Scout did not produce the frame-by-frame evidence required for a controlled-flame reassessment. A prompt change alone has not made that path reliable. The current local YOLO checkpoint recognizes fire/smoke only; no independently evaluated local lighter/secondary-burning model is installed. Initial live confirmation therefore remains urgent by design. The readiness evaluator now blocks unless a real frozen edge-model bundle passes development checks and the held-out results match its hash. The development camera policy used in the cloud runs is an expiring test fixture, not a reviewed real installation.

The deployed evaluation Worker demonstrates PC-to-Cloudflare transport, incident persistence and real Scout integration. The production `fire-guard-api` Worker has not been deployed. Its offline configuration preflight and bundle dry-run passed, but a read-only remote D1 migration check found `0002_analysis.sql`, `0003_event_keys.sql` and `0004_operator_reviews.sql` pending. Production ingest secret/operator configuration and automated allowance reconciliation are not established. A production deployment from the current default profile would leave the experimental lighter-priority flags off and cannot be presented as a fix for the priority requirement.

## Minimum remaining release work

1. Freeze independently reviewed controlled-lighter, lighter-to-material, material-burning, smoke, reflection and obscured recordings with distinct development and held-out sources. The Desktop test folder contains the same three development clips; it is not held-out evidence. The formal handheld corpus currently has **zero reviewed development clips, zero held-out clips and zero coverage in every required category**.
2. Build and validate a local edge classifier/track for lighter-tip flame versus secondary burning, or demonstrate an alternative with equivalent measured reliability and latency. A research candidate, [DetectiumFire](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02495), reports 1,075 lighter-flame images, but those images do not supply this product’s held-out video transitions. Its paper says the web-search data is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and limits that material to academic use; the exact licensing of any candidate subset and associated weights must be checked before product use. Do not treat it as an approved shipping model. The post-confirmation Scout evidence pack has now been improved to send a fresh, time-spread sequence; fresh cloud behavior still needs a real replay. Keep smoke, secondary fire, unclear images, stale evidence and reviewed site hazards as urgent vetoes. Do not lower live priority based on a word in Scout output.
3. Re-run the full PC-to-deployed-Cloudflare corpus with fresh Scout where needed; require controlled-use monitoring, actionable-fire urgency, complete D1/event readback, and measured false urgent alerts and misses. Preserve model responses and account neuron totals.
4. Configure a real camera policy, production secret and operator access; apply/read back production D1 migrations; establish automatic account-wide allowance refresh or an explicit fail-closed operational procedure. Deploy production only after its actual inference path has been exercised.
5. Install on the actual Raspberry Pi and measure sustained capture/inference throughput, thermals, offline storage/recovery, network loss, restart behavior and notification delivery. PC results do not establish Pi performance.

Until those gates pass, the honest delivery is a supervised advisory pilot that treats uncertain or confirmed flames as urgent. It must not be described as a validated automatic danger score or a certified fire alarm.
